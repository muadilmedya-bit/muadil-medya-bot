## 2. The Evolution of Hard Disk Drives

Hard disk drives use magnetic storage with rapidly rotating platters and moving actuator arms to read and write data. IBM introduced the first commercial disk drive, the 350, in 1956 as part of the 305 RAMAC system. Hard disk drives remain non-volatile storage, retaining data when powered off and dominating exabytes of server-side volume.

## 3. The Shift from HDDs to Solid-State Storage

As technology advanced, we saw a major shift from traditional hard disk drives to solid-state storage. Solid-state drives, or SSDs, primarily use NAND flash memory. Compared to older hard disk drives, SSDs feature much higher data-transfer rates and significantly lower latency and access times. Because speed, power consumption, small size, high capacity, and durability are important for modern applications, SSDs began replacing hard disk drives where those factors matter most. This transition happened rapidly on a commercial scale. In fact, flash storage products had more than twice the revenue of hard disk drives as of 2017, and the revenues for SSDs slightly exceeded those for hard disks in 2018. While personal computing devices produced in large volumes, like mobile phones and tablets, rely heavily on flash memory storage, the ongoing evolution of storage hardware continues to shape how we secure and maintain our digital lives.

## 4. Understanding Storage Capacity and Performance

When evaluating hard disk drives, the primary characteristics to consider are capacity and performance. Hard disk drive capacity is specified using unit prefixes that correspond to powers of 1000. For instance, a one-terabyte drive has a capacity of one thousand gigabytes, where one gigabyte equals one thousand megabytes, which equals one million kilobytes, or one billion bytes. Typically, some of this capacity is unavailable to the user because it is used by the file system and the computer operating system, and possibly inbuilt redundancy for error correction and recovery. Furthermore, there can be confusion regarding storage capacity because manufacturers state capacities in decimal gigabytes using powers of 1000, whereas the most commonly used operating systems report capacities in powers of 1024, which results in a smaller number than advertised. Performance is measured by average access time, which is the time required to move the heads to a track or cylinder, average rotational latency, which is the time it takes for the desired sector to move under the head as a function of physical rotational speed in revolutions per minute, and the data transmission rate, which is the speed at which the data is transmitted. As for physical builds, the common modern hard disk drive form factors are 3.5-inch for desktop computers, and 2.5-inch, which is used primarily for laptops and servers.

## 5. Securing Data Through Disk Encryption

Disk encryption is a technology that protects information by converting it into code that cannot be deciphered easily by unauthorized people or processes. It uses disk encryption software or hardware to encrypt every bit of data that goes on a disk or disk volume, which is used to prevent unauthorized access to data storage. Full disk encryption, or whole disk encryption, signifies that everything on the disk is encrypted, barring foundational areas like the master boot record with code that starts the operating system loading sequence, though some hardware-based systems can truly encrypt an entire boot disk. To manage this seamlessly, transparent encryption, also known as real-time encryption or on-the-fly encryption, is a method used by some disk encryption software where data is automatically encrypted or decrypted as it is saved or loaded.

## 6. Hardware Protections and Trusted Platform Modules

When looking at hardware protections, Trusted Platform Modules, or TPMs, play a critical role. A Trusted Platform Module is a secure cryptoprocessor embedded in the motherboard that can be used to authenticate a hardware device. Because each TPM chip is unique to a particular device, it is capable of performing platform authentication to verify that the system seeking access is the expected system. A limited number of disk encryption solutions have support for this technology. These implementations can wrap the decryption key using the TPM, thus tying the hard disk drive to a particular device. If the hard disk drive is removed from that particular device and placed in another, the decryption process will fail. Furthermore, the TPM can impose a limit on decryption attempts per unit time, making brute-forcing harder. Beyond motherboards and secure cryptoprocessors, hardware-based full disk encryption within the storage device itself provides another layer of protection. These are called self-encrypting drives, and they have no impact on performance whatsoever. In these devices, the media-encryption key never leaves the device itself and is therefore not available to any malware operating within the system.

## 7. The Cloud Storage Model and Its Giants

Cloud storage keeps data in remote logical pools managed by third-party cloud computing providers. People and organizations buy or lease storage capacity from these providers to store user, organization, or application data. Major global cloud providers include AWS, Google, Microsoft, Oracle, Alibaba, IBM, Huawei, and Tencent. These cloud storage providers are responsible for keeping the data available and accessible, and the physical environment secured, protected, and running. Cloud storage relies on virtualized infrastructure with high elasticity, scalability, and multi-tenancy. The physical storage spans multiple servers, sometimes in multiple locations, and the physical environment is typically owned and managed by a cloud computing provider. Cloud storage services may be accessed through a colocated cloud computing service, a web service application programming interface, or by applications that use the API, such as cloud desktop storage, a cloud storage gateway, or web-based content management systems.

## 8. The Origins and Growth of Cloud Computing

The conceptual foundation of cloud computing dates back to the nineteen sixties with the work of J.C.R. Licklider on ARPANET. His vision was to connect people and data from anywhere at any time. Decades later, the National Institute of Standards and Technology, or NIST, defined five essential cloud characteristics that shaped modern infrastructure. These include on-demand self-service, broad network access, and resource pooling, alongside rapid elasticity and measured service. Building on these foundational principles, modern cloud providers now offer scalable compute resources, managed services, and integrated artificial intelligence tools that help organizations manage complex data storage and processing needs without maintaining heavy physical hardware on site.

## 9. Preservation Standards and the OAIS Model

When preserving digital data for the long term, organizations rely on the Open Archival Information System reference model, which is standardized as ISO 14721:2025. While initially developed by the Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems with space agencies in mind, the OAIS model has proved useful to other organizations and institutions with digital archiving needs. It sets the framework for long term preservation and managing impacts from changing technologies, including support for new media and data formats, or with a changing user community, which is any group of consumers capable of understanding the information. To achieve this, the OAIS model defines specific information packages. These include the Submission Information Package, which is the information sent from the producer to the archive, the Archival Information Package, which is the information stored by the archive, and the Dissemination Information Package, which is the information sent to a user when requested. These three information packages may or may not be identical to each other.

## 10. The Intersection of Digital Marketing and Data

Digital marketing emerged in the 1990s as computers gained the capacity to store large volumes of customer information. During the 1980s, the storage capacity of computers was already large enough to store this information, allowing companies to choose online techniques such as database marketing rather than relying on limited list brokers. These databases transformed the relationship between buyers and sellers by letting companies track customer information more effectively. As digital media grew, it led to billions of online ads and the widespread use of online behavioral advertising. An increasing portion of advertising stems from businesses employing online behavioral advertising to tailor ads for internet users, though this practice raises concerns about consumer privacy and data protection. In the modern era, digital marketing increasingly incorporates artificial intelligence-generated responses. As these tools began to be offered as a replacement for simple search engine results, digital marketers developed methods known as generative engine optimization or answer engine optimization to market via these tools.

## 11. Summary and the Takeaway for Digital Preservation

Long term data preservation requires strict adherence to archival frameworks like the OAIS reference model. Never rely on a single physical storage medium.
